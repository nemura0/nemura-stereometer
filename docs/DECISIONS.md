| 2026-11-21 | Transport slots | A slot's state is its 64-bit owner word: 0 = free, else the owner's instance id, whose high 32 bits are the owner's pid (the low 32: a per-process counter from a random start). Claim, release and reclaim are single compare-exchanges on it. Only the owner writes the description (pid, start time, rate, channels, name, ring name) under a sequence lock, and the description names its owner; heartbeats and rings are written only by the slot's current owner (`beat` and `Ring::create` check it). Stale = no heartbeat for 1 s and the owner process gone (pid + start time via sysctl; zombies count as dead): the app reclaims it, and a claim takes it when no slot is free. A live owner whose host stopped processing keeps its slot; it is only left out of the list. | A separate state field would be a second atomic that a reclaim could not update together with the owner, and a claimer that crashed between the two writes would leave a slot nobody can free. With the pid in the word, a crash at any point (even mid-description) leaves a reclaimable slot; tested with fork, zombies and a stuck sequence. Requiring silence as well means a process lookup the system answers wrongly (a sandbox) never takes a slot that is still sending. |
| 2026-11-21 | Transport rings | Rings keep their write counter across owners (readers never see it go back). A slot's new owner clears the frames, prefaults every page and wires it with `mlock` (best effort), then publishes itself in the ring header (release), so a reader that sees the new owner also sees the cleared frames and its sample rate. The header also records the channel count. A registry of another version is refused; a smaller ring left by another version is replaced by the slot's new owner. | No page faults on the audio thread; the app can tell whose ring it is reading. Write + publish of 256 frames, owner-checked heartbeat included, measured at p50 83 ns / p99 125–167 ns (arm64) and 42 / 125 ns (x86_64 under Rosetta), budget 500 ns / 2 µs. |
| 2026-11-21 | DSP | Mapping: M/S X = (R − L)/2, Y = (L + R)/2, so full scale is the unit diamond; L/R is (Y − X, Y + X); the log scale turns the M/S radius r into 1 + 20·log10(r)/range (clamped to 0…1) and keeps the direction; the half view mirrors points below the axis through the center, then stretches Y over the 2:1 view. Frequency color: each channel is split at 250 Hz and 3 kHz (second-order Butterworth, double precision), both channels' band levels are smoothed over 15 ms and weighted 1 / 1.6 / 2.5, t = (mids/2 + highs)/total, held below −140 dB. Meters: exponential averages (double) over the integration time; correlation and balance read 0 below −120 dBFS. Auto-gain: the loudest recent sample's M/S radius goes to −3 dB; the held peak falls 6 dB/s (fast) or 2 dB/s (slow) and the gain follows it with a 0.2 s or 1.5 s time constant; samples under −72 dBFS are skipped. The analyzer turns NaN/Inf samples into silence, clamps samples to ±1000 (+60 dBFS) and clamps settings and sample rates (8–768 kHz). | The brief fixes the modes, ranges and test cases, not the formulas. Mirroring through the center (not across the axis) keeps right-channel content on the right in the half view. Summing both channels' band levels gives side-only content its own color. The held peak makes auto-gain follow the loudest moments rather than the average (tested), and skipping quiet samples keeps a pause between songs from pushing the gain to +36 dB. Constants get tuned against real music at Checkpoint B. |
